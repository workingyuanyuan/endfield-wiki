### HUMAN RESOURCES SUMMARY

> Operator Kay, real name Cerube, is a reconvener from Rhodes Island. Under Warfarin's instruction, she left Rhodes Island to journey across Talos-II. 
>
> Kay joining Endfield wasn't entirely a coincidence. In fact, Endfield was the final destination of her journey. More than half a year ago, we received word from Rhodes Island: a young reconvener had already set out from Rhodes Island and would arrive at Endfield to report in shortly. "Shortly" meaning this very moment, one hundred eighty-five days, eight hours, twelve minutes, and fifty-five seconds later. My retrieval system took nearly half a second to respond before I found her name in the personnel to-do list. \
> As for her greatly delayed reporting, she explained: she had left Rhodes Island precisely to find the Endministrator. In Warfarin's stories, the Endministrator had always remained aboard Dijiang. So she traveled across the surface of Talos-II, searching for an aerospace work vessel called "Dijiang". If she hadn't happened to run into the Endministrator in the outskirts of Wuling, she probably would have kept searching. \
> Having traveled alone across Talos-II for so long and still appearing before us safe and sound, at least by this alone, she is unquestionably an expert in wilderness travel. \
> — Martin Marvin Malen, Assistant, HR Division, Endfield Industries

### FILE 1

> When asked what codename she wished to use while operating at Endfield, Cerube simply chose the name her friends called her: "Kay". \
> At Endfield, her way of handling things can indeed be simple enough. \
> She doesn't try to understand complex administrative hierarchies or combat sequences. After spending some time with everyone at Endfield, everyone was simply sorted by her into three major categories: "good people who can give me food", "strong people who can fight with me", and "very busy people who don't have much time". This minimalistic interpersonal logic actually made her seem especially relaxed in the tense and orderly life, and it also allowed her to quickly make quite a few friends after arriving at Endfield. With her blunt simplicity and seemingly inexhaustible energy, who could bear to refuse her? \
> So you can see Kay darting around among the various operators. She'll excitedly drag an operator who just came out of the training room to visit a "secret base" she newly discovered: usually some observation window with a view of Talos-II, or a little castle built from old parts behind the engineering division. You can also often see her sharing a new ice cream dessert with Laevatain, or spending an entire day holed up in the weapons testing room with Yvonne. Occasionally, you can also see her being caught on the spot by logistics operators and stopped outside the door after trying to break into the food preparation area of the canteen. \
> Kay interacts with everyone in the simplest way, and the operators often choose the simplest way to repay her friendliness. Everyone discovered that Kay can be said to never turn down any kind of food, so over time, her pack became stuffed with all sorts of foods with peculiar flavors. Until one day, her backpack full of snacks triggered a warning during boarding inspection due to being overweight. Since then, on Kay's suggestion, all the snacks given to her were handed over to Perlica to supervise and keep on her behalf. \
> "Don't get any funny ideas. Don't think that just because you feed her, you'll be able to win the next eating contest... You can't feed her enough."

### FILE 2

*Reach trust: 300*

> Having been with Endfield for so long, I've never seen an operator so enthusiastic about field missions. \
> Talos-II's environment is complex and ever-changing, and enemies often become troublesome too. The dangers go without saying, and the heavy responsibility also puts most operators under considerable pressure. But for operator Kay, field missions are more like officially approved expeditions. Sometimes, the moment she's added to the personnel list, she'll come running to confirm the departure time, with an eagerness in her tone that she clearly can't hide. Compared to the enclosed Dijiang, the vast world of Talos-II seems to be the playground Kay prefers. But to this day, I still can't understand... After such a long journey, how can she still maintain such intense curiosity about everything around her? \
> Because of her long experience traveling in the wilderness, Kay often serves as a guide during missions. But when out in the field with her, she always keeps testing the edge of leaving the group: sniffing here, poking around there, and if you don't pay attention for even a moment, she'll disappear from everyone's sight. The amazing thing is, with just one call, you'll hear her respond from behind some nearby bush, and you'll find she was actually right there beside you all along. Then she'll bring back a pebble shaped remarkably like Dijiang, or a brightly colored flower of unknown name but with an unusually pleasant scent. \
> Oh, please don't misunderstand. She won't neglect her duties as a guide just because she's playful. I even think the reason she can constantly maintain awareness of her surroundings is precisely because she always keeps herself highly energized mentally. She often senses changes in the surrounding environment earlier than anyone else in the team, and immediately enters combat mode with high morale. When encountering sudden situations, she can even warn of enemy movements before the sensors respond, or tell us the exact location of danger. And when asked how she actually does it, she'll only give answers like "the smell of the air is wrong" or "that direction feels uncomfy". If this isn't some kind of Originium Arts, then it's probably intuition and premonition honed from long years of roaming Talos-II.

### FILE 4

*Reach trust: 1500*

> Ever since she woke up on Rhodes Island, Kay stayed aboard the ship for a long time. Rhodes Island seemed no different from before. The operators all had their own missions, and many weren't often on the ship. No one ever assigned her any particular duties. She only needed to train, sleep well, and eat well, just as she had originally wished. Sometimes she would go find Warfarin and listen to her tell stories about this planet, about Terra, and about Kay herself. She could stare blankly at the scenery outside the porthole for ages, as it changed from snowy mountains to forests, then to deserts, and then back to snowy mountains. It seemed that as long as she was willing, this kind of time would never end. \
> Until one day, she decided to pack her bags. \
> She sat before the porthole for a long time, her ears twitching slightly, as if catching a sound echoing by her ear. The stories Warfarin had told slowly linked together in her mind into a road. At the end of that road was a place called "Endfield". \
> She didn't know what that really meant. An endpoint? A place to belong? Or some kind of answer waiting for her? She couldn't figure it out, but she vaguely felt that something there was calling to her. \
> And so she stood. \
> The preparation process felt more like a fun game to her. She picked the biggest, sturdiest backpack and stuffed it with all kinds of snacks and dry rations, honey waffles in particular took the most important spot. Then she tried summoning a few handy weapons, watching them appear and fade away around her, and nodded in satisfaction. She was ready. \
> Warfarin seemed to have anticipated her departure. She handed over a small extra bag of honey waffles. "If you get lost," Warfarin said, "the Endmin will tell you which way to go." \
> She nodded earnestly, though she wasn't sure what "getting lost" felt like. This was her first time setting out to explore this brand-new world. \
> On the day of departure, she walked to the cabin door with her bulging pack on her back. The door slowly opened, and the wind outside rushed in, carrying unfamiliar scents. Talos-II lay before her. \
> "Endfield..." She softly repeated the name, as if confirming her direction. \
> Then she took her first step. \
> She didn't know what lay ahead, nor how long this journey would last, nor how many times in the future she would set out on journeys like this. But she vaguely understood that setting out on a journey wasn't just to find delicious things she hadn't tasted, nor just to see scenery she hadn't seen before— \
> She was searching for that feeling that could only be found on a journey. \
> One that was called "going home".
